5 Easy Plants for Kids to Grow (Ages 2–6)

August 5, 2026

TL;DR

Sunflowers, snap peas, radishes, basil, and marigolds are the five easiest plants for kids ages 2–6 to grow, because each sprouts within about a week and shows visible daily change — the two traits that keep a young child's interest.

Not every plant is a good first plant. Slow growers lose a toddler's interest before anything visible happens. These five were picked specifically for speed and visibility, not because they're the "best" plants in general.

What makes a plant kid-friendly

Two things matter more than anything else: how fast it sprouts, and how obvious the daily change is. A plant that takes three weeks to show anything will lose a 4-year-old's attention by day two. A plant that visibly grows a little taller or fuller every single day turns "checking on the plant" into a daily habit instead of a chore.

The five plants

Sunflowers — sprout in about a week, then grow noticeably taller every few days. The payoff (an actual flower) is dramatic enough to be worth the wait.

Snap peas — fast sprouters with curling tendrils kids can watch reach for support, which adds a bit of "look what it did today" excitement.

Radishes — one of the fastest edible plants around, often ready to harvest in under a month, which gives kids an end goal they can actually eat.

Basil — grows fast and smells strong enough that kids notice it with their nose, not just their eyes, adding a sensory element.

Marigolds — nearly foolproof and bloom in bright, kid-pleasing orange and yellow, good for a low-stakes first flower.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest plant for a toddler to grow?
Sunflowers are typically the easiest for toddlers because they sprout within about a week and grow visibly taller every few days, which holds a young child's attention far better than slower-growing plants.
How long does it take for kid-friendly plants to sprout?
Most beginner-friendly plants like sunflowers, radishes, and snap peas sprout within 5 to 10 days under normal indoor conditions, which is fast enough to keep a young child engaged.
Do these plants need special soil or containers for kids?
No special soil is required — standard potting mix works for all five plants. A wide, tip-resistant pot is more important than soil type, since it lets small children water and check on the plant without knocking it over.
